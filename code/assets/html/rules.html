Classic Checkers

Checkers is a game for two players, played on the 32 dark squares of a standard 8×8 board. Each player has twelve round pieces, called men, which begin on the three rows nearest to them. Red moves first, and the two players alternate until one of them cannot move, or has no pieces left.

The aim

The aim is to take every one of your opponent's pieces off the board, or to leave them with no legal move at all. Whoever is reduced to kings alone against a side that still has men is normally declared the loser straight away.

Moving

  • On your turn you must move exactly one piece.
  • A man moves one square diagonally forwards, towards the far edge of the board. It never moves sideways, and under American rules it never moves backwards.
  • A king is crowned when a man reaches the row of squares at the far end of the board. A king moves any number of empty squares in one diagonal direction, like a bishop in chess.

Capturing

You capture by jumping over one of your opponent's pieces into the empty square immediately beyond it. Only pieces already on the board are jumped, and your own pieces can be neither jumped nor landed on.

  • If a capture is available you must take it. A quiet move is only allowed when nothing can be captured.
  • When several captures are available you must play the one that takes the most pieces. If two moves take the same number, take the one that leads to a further capture.
  • Under American rules a man may capture in any direction, forwards or backwards. Under British rules it may only capture forwards.
  • Several captures may be chained together in a single move, provided the same piece makes all of them and no piece is taken twice.

Winning a game

A game is won when:

  • all of your opponent's pieces have been captured, or
  • your opponent has no legal move left, or
  • your opponent has been reduced to kings only while you still have men, or
  • your opponent resigns.

Draws

Under the American rules a game is drawn when the same position occurs three times with the same player to move, or when fifty moves by each player pass with no capture and no promotion. A game is also drawn when both players are left with kings only.

Notation

Games are written down so that they can be replayed. A square is named by a letter for its column, from a to h, followed by a number for its row, from 1 to 8. Red's back row is row 1.

  • A quiet move is written with a dash, for example b2-b4.
  • A capture is written with an x for every jump, for example b2xd4xf6 for a double capture.
  • A move that crowns a piece is marked with an asterisk, for example c7-b8*.
